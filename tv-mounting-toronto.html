Every wall, every install

Brick, concrete or drywall — the price does not change

Toronto housing stock is a mixed bag and the wall behind your TV does not care what the rate card says. We carry anchors for drywall on wood or steel studs, brick veneer, poured concrete and stone — including the concrete columns that show up behind half the TVs in this city’s condos. Same flat price, whatever is back there; only the anchor changes.

Mount: supply your own and we will confirm it fits the TV and the wall before anything gets drilled, or tell us the size and we will bring one at cost, receipt shown. Fixed mounts are fine for a TV you watch head-on; anywhere you will be looking up or across — over a fireplace, in a kitchen, off to the side of a sectional — a full-motion arm earns its keep, which is part of why that tier is $219.

Over the fireplace: the mount goes high enough that the panel is not sitting in the heat coming off the mantel, cable stays clear of the flue, and power runs through an in-wall kit instead of a cord taped to the brick. In-wall cable concealment is the same idea applied to any wall — HDMI and speaker cable can run inside drywall with no special hardware, but power needs a code-rated kit with its own outlet box behind the TV, not a bare extension cord. That distinction is the whole $79.

Toronto FAQ

Straight answers about TV mounting in Toronto

How much does TV mounting cost in Toronto?
$129 flat for a standard mount up to 55″, $169 for 56″–75″, and $219 for 76″ and up, over a fireplace, or a full-motion arm. In-wall cable concealment is +$79 and a soundbar install is +$49. That is the whole list — the price we quote by text is the price on the invoice.
Is it safe to mount a TV over a fireplace?
Yes, with the right hardware. We use a full-motion or heat-rated fixed mount set high enough that the panel is not sitting in the heat plume, keep the low-voltage cables clear of the flue, and route power through a code-rated in-wall kit rather than a cord draped down the brick. Gas fireplaces are rarely a problem; wood-burning units get a clearance check before anything goes on the wall.
Do I need to buy my own TV mount, or do you supply one?
Either. Bring your own and we will confirm it fits the TV and the wall before we drill, or tell us the size and viewing angle and we will supply the right mount at cost, receipt included. Over a fireplace or in a room where you will watch from an angle, we usually recommend a full-motion arm rather than fixed.
What is in-wall cable concealment, and is it actually code-safe?
It is routing the HDMI and speaker cable inside the wall so nothing hangs down to the console — that part is always fine, low-voltage cable is allowed in-wall with no special kit. Power is the one people get wrong: a bare extension cord behind drywall is a fire code violation. We use a certified in-wall power kit with its own outlet box behind the TV, which is the code-safe way to lose the cord.
I am in a condo or a rental — can you still mount on my walls?
Yes. Most Toronto condo walls are poured concrete, which we drill routinely — it just takes different anchors, same price. For rentals, check your lease or condo board rules before we book; we only drill with your go-ahead, and we use the minimum number of holes the mount actually needs, not a template’s worth.
How long does a TV mounting install take?
A standard wall mount is usually 45 minutes to an hour and a half. Add 30–45 minutes for in-wall cable concealment, and a bit more for an over-fireplace or full-motion job. Zone A bookings are often same-week, and same-day is common if you catch us on a light day.
