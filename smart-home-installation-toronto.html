The difference that matters

"Paired an app" and "programmed a system" are not the same job

Most smart home installs in the GTA amount to someone plugging in a hub, downloading the manufacturer's app, and handing you three different apps depending on which brand you bought. That is pairing. It works, technically, for about as long as nobody touches the router. Programming is different: your lights, shades, thermostat, cameras and locks get reduced to a small set of scenes and schedules that behave the way you actually live — triggered from whichever app, voice assistant or wall switch you already reach for, not the one that was easiest to set up.

That difference is why the flagship credential on this page is not "smart home installer." It is certified AV field engineer & control-system programmer — trained on commercial AV systems and Bell installs before moving into residential work. A control-system programmer thinks in states, triggers and failure modes, the same way a commercial building's lighting and AV system is programmed to survive a power blip or a network reboot without someone driving out to reset it. That is what gets built into a $499 starter, not just a five-device demo.

We are also ecosystem-agnostic. We do not steer you into a platform because it is easier for us — we build on top of whichever ecosystem you already have phones for, or whichever one makes sense for the devices you already own: Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, SmartThings, or a mix that talks to each other through Matter and Thread where the hardware supports it. If your household is split between iPhones and Android, we will say so up front, not sell you a system that only half the house can use.

And this is retrofit-friendly work. We are not asking you to open walls to run low-voltage cable for a house that was framed and drywalled thirty years ago. Almost everything we install runs on Wi-Fi, Zigbee or Thread and mounts into the switch boxes and outlets already in the wall. Where a real wire is unavoidable — a hardwired thermostat with no C-wire, a large motorized shade, in-ceiling audio — we flag it on the walkthrough, before anything gets drilled, and it shows up in the quote in writing, not as a surprise at the door.

What we install

Lighting, shades, climate, cameras and audio — one system, not six apps

Lighting — smart switches, dimmers and bulbs, grouped into rooms and scenes instead of forty individual entries in an app. Motorized shades and blinds — retrofit motors on shades you already own where possible, or new shades where they are not, scheduled to the sun rather than a fixed clock. Thermostats and climate — smart thermostats and, where the system supports it, per-room sensors so the thermostat is not guessing based on the hallway.

Cameras and video doorbells — mounted, angled, connected to your Wi-Fi or a recorder, and folded into the same scenes as everything else (porch light triggers off the doorbell, cameras arm when the last phone leaves the geofence). If cameras and recording are the whole job rather than one piece of a bigger system, our dedicated security camera installation in Toronto page has the full rate card for that. Multi-room audio — speakers and streaming amps zoned by room and grouped into the same control app as the lights. For a dedicated cinema room or a full home theatre build, see home theatre installation in Toronto. Smart hubs — the box that ties all of the above together, chosen for what you already own rather than what we would prefer to sell.

Every job leaves with documentation: what is on the hub, which devices belong to which scene, and what the wiring looks like where we touched it. That is not a courtesy — it means the next tech, or you at 11pm with a screwdriver, can actually understand what is behind the wall plate instead of guessing.

Toronto FAQ

Straight answers about smart home installation

What's actually included in the $499 starter?
A hub and five devices — any mix of smart switches, plugs, thermostats, locks, sensors or a camera — mounted, wired if needed, connected to the hub, and programmed into two or three real scenes: a morning routine, an away mode, a goodnight scene, whatever fits how you live. Not just installed, tested and demoed before we leave. Extra devices are quoted per device once we know the room count.
Which ecosystem should I pick — Apple, Google or Alexa?
Whichever one you already have phones for, honestly. We are ecosystem-agnostic — we build the automation on top of Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa or SmartThings, using devices that support the standard properly rather than half-heartedly. If your household is split between iPhones and Android, we will say so up front and pick the option that causes the least friction, not the one that is easiest for us.
Will it keep working after you leave, and what if something breaks?
That is the point of hiring a control-system programmer instead of someone who pairs an app. The scenes and schedules are built to survive a Wi-Fi reboot, a firmware update or a dead battery without falling over, and everything is documented so a fix does not mean starting from scratch. Every mount and wiring job carries a lifetime workmanship warranty — if something we installed comes loose or fails, we come back and fix it free. Devices themselves carry the manufacturer's warranty.
Can you use the devices I already bought?
Yes, that is usually where we start. Text us the brands and models you already own and we will tell you honestly what we can integrate, what needs a firmware update first, and what should be returned because it will fight the rest of the system. You are not required to buy anything through us — bring your own hardware and we bill only for the labour.
Do I need to rewire the house for this?
Almost never. Most of what we install runs on Wi-Fi, Zigbee or Thread and mounts to existing switch boxes and outlets. A handful of jobs — in-ceiling audio, motorized shades on a large window, a hardwired thermostat with no C-wire — need a short cable run or a neutral wire pulled, and we will flag that on the walkthrough before any drilling starts, not after.
What drives the cost of a whole-home automation quote?
Device count first, then how many of them need real wiring — motorized shades and in-ceiling audio cost more in labour than a plug-in smart switch. After that it is scene complexity: three rooms on a schedule is a different job from a whole house that reacts to who is home, the time of day and the weather. We write the quote after a walkthrough or a detailed video call, and it does not move once it is in writing.
