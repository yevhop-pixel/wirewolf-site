Who this is for

The honest answer about Starlink in Milton — depends which side of town you're on

Central Milton is not short on internet. Fibre and cable run through most of the newer subdivisions south of the highway, and if that's where you live and it's working, we're not going to talk you into a $449 Starlink install you don't need — we'll say so on the phone before you book anything. The properties we actually go out to sit north and west of that line: up on the escarpment above town, out through Campbellville and Brookville, along the Nassagaweya concessions, and the stretch of Halton Hills between Georgetown and Acton where the fibre map is a lot more confident than the actual line at the end of the driveway.

That rural belt is bigger than the postal code suggests. Nassagaweya alone covers a grid of concessions where cable simply stops — you can be five minutes from a serviced subdivision and still be looking at a phone line from the 1990s. Kilbride and Moffat sit in the same spot, close enough to town that people assume they're covered and far enough out that they're not. Same story in Halton Hills: Georgetown's built-up core has fibre, and the properties strung out toward Acton and the open country between often don't.

Three things come up constantly on these jobs. Escarpment terrain. A lot of the ridge properties above Milton and around Campbellville sit right on the Niagara Escarpment, and between the drop and the tree cover a roof mount can't always see enough open sky — we survey first and move to a pole mount when it needs it. Hobby farms and horse properties, which are common out this way — the house gets a clean signal and the barn forty metres off gets nothing, because Wi-Fi doesn't travel through a hundred feet of hay and steel siding; that's an Ethernet drop, not a stronger router. And new-build estate lots on the fringe of the serviced area, where the subdivision two streets over has fibre and the new build on five acres does not, because nobody told the buyer that before they closed.

What makes us different

The dish is step one. Whole-property Wi-Fi is the actual job.

Most Starlink installers are dish installers, full stop. They get the kit, run a cable, mount it, point it at a clear patch of sky, and leave once the app shows green. That's a fine afternoon for whoever ordered the kit and a bad one for whoever tries to get online from the bedroom at the far end of the house, or the shop out back, or the loft over the garage. We hear the same complaint on almost every callback job, regardless of who installed the original dish: the signal doesn't reach past one room.

Wirewolf isn't a dish company that also does networking — it's the other way around. We're an AV and control-systems outfit first: certified field engineer, control-system programmer, years running satellite and rural install work for Bell before Starlink existed. The dish is the easy 20 minutes. What actually determines whether your property has usable internet is everything after it — mesh nodes placed by someone who understands RF, not just distance; PoE access points where a plug-in extender won't cut it; proper Ethernet runs to the barn, the shop, the bunkie, the second building, instead of hoping a signal punches through steel and hay; cable dressed and secured along a wall instead of zip-tied wherever it happened to reach.

A recent Milton job makes the point. Two houses on one rural property, each needing its own Starlink — standard roof mounts on both, no pole needed once we checked the sky view. Wiring the second house properly meant a 150 ft custom cable run, clipped along the facade and routed inside rather than drilled through wherever was convenient, plus three mesh Wi-Fi nodes split across both buildings for coverage that actually reaches every room, not just the one closest to the dish. That's the job in full: two dishes, one clean cable run, and a network that covers the whole property instead of half of it.

Milton FAQ

Straight answers about Starlink in Milton

How much does Starlink installation cost in Milton?
$449 flat for a standard roof or gable mount, or $549 for a pole or non-penetrating mount. Milton sits inside our Zone A, so travel is free — no add-on, no surprise line item. The number is confirmed in writing before we book, and it doesn't change at your door.
Do I actually need Starlink if I live in Milton?
If you're in one of the newer subdivisions with working fibre or cable, no — Starlink isn't an upgrade, and we'll say so outright. Out in Campbellville, Brookville, Kilbride, Moffat, along the Nassagaweya concessions, or in the rural stretch of Halton Hills around Georgetown and Acton, the answer is usually yes: the coverage map says serviced, and the actual signal at the house says otherwise.
I'm on the escarpment or have a lot of trees — does that rule Starlink out?
Not usually, but it changes the mount. A roof mount only works if the dish gets a clear view of open sky, and the ridge properties above Milton and around Campbellville — plus mature tree cover on a lot of escarpment lots — often block that from the roofline. We check the sky view first and move to a pole or non-penetrating mount ($549) when the roof can't clear the tree line.
Can you cover a big property or two buildings with Wi-Fi?
Yes — that's most of the actual work on a rural install. A recent Milton job had two houses on one property: Starlink on both, standard roof mounts, a 150 ft custom cable run clipped along the facade and routed inside to reach the second building properly, and three mesh Wi-Fi nodes split across both houses for coverage that reaches every room instead of just the one closest to the dish.
How soon can you come to Milton?
Weekend slots book first and usually go within the week. Milton is inside our free Zone A, so there's no travel fee working against the schedule, and if you and a neighbour both need an install, ask us to pair the visit on one trip — that's when the $100 two-install discount applies.
