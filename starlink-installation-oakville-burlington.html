Who this is for

The honest answer about Starlink in Oakville and Burlington — it depends where you are

Most of Oakville and most of Burlington do not need Starlink. Cable and fibre run through the built-up subdivisions on both sides of the QEW and the 403, and if that is where you live and the connection works, we are not going to talk you into a $449 install you don't need. That's most of the calls we could take and don't — for a wider look at when a satellite line actually makes sense, see our rural internet in Ontario guide.

The real work sits north of Dundas St and the 407 — Kilbride, Lowville, the concessions running through north Burlington, and the rural estates scattered across Halton beyond the last fibre node. Out there the provider's coverage map is a lot more optimistic than the actual signal at the end of a half-kilometre driveway, and Starlink is a legitimate first connection, not a novelty.

Two more patterns come up. Bronte, Palermo and the ravine lots along the escarpment edge often have cable running to the house and still can't get Starlink working from a roof mount, because mature trees block the clear sky view a dish needs — that's a pole-mount job, not a coverage problem. On the big estate lots common through Glen Abbey and further out, the house sits far enough from the road that whatever Wi-Fi is already inside dies before it reaches the pool house or the far end of the yard — a network problem, not an internet-source problem. And some clients have working cable and want Starlink anyway, as a wired-in backup for a home office where a dropped call actually costs something.

Oakville & Burlington FAQ

Straight answers about Starlink in Oakville and Burlington

How much does Starlink installation cost in Oakville or Burlington?
$449 flat for a standard roof or gable mount, or $549 for a pole or non-penetrating mount. Oakville sits in our Zone A, so travel is free. Burlington is Zone B — add a $59 travel fee. The number is confirmed in writing before we book, and it does not change at your door.
Do I actually need Starlink if I live in Oakville or Burlington?
Honestly, most of the time no. Oakville and Burlington are well served by cable and fibre, and if that connection works, Starlink is not an upgrade — we'll say so. It earns its keep north of Dundas St and the 407, in Kilbride, Lowville and the rural concessions of north Burlington, where the coverage map is more confident than the actual signal. It's also legitimate as a backup connection for a home office, run alongside your existing cable.
I've got a big lot with heavy tree cover — does that rule Starlink out?
Not usually, but it changes the mount. A roof mount needs a clear view of open sky, and lakefront or ravine tree cover — common in Bronte, Palermo and along the escarpment edge — often blocks that from the roofline. On large estate lots set back from the road, the bigger issue is usually Wi-Fi range once the dish is up, not the dish itself. We survey the sky view first and move to a pole or non-penetrating mount ($549) when the roof can't clear the trees.
Can you get real Wi-Fi to a pool house or backyard office?
Yes — this is most of the actual job on big Oakville and Burlington properties. Ethernet runs are $120 per drop to a pool house, coach house, detached garage or backyard office, with PoE access points and mesh Wi-Fi (from $199 per node) so the connection is solid, not a repeater guessing through a hedge.
Can I keep my existing cable and use Starlink as a backup?
Yes. A lot of our Oakville and Burlington clients run Starlink alongside Cogeco or Bell rather than instead of it, specifically for a home office that can't afford downtime. We install it as a genuine second line, wired into your network so it's there the moment the primary connection drops.
