Local housing, local tools

Concrete condos and century homes need different tools, not the same drill bit

Concrete condo towers in Humber Bay Shores and Islington-City Centre West are poured concrete or concrete block behind the drywall skin, so a standard drywall anchor does nothing. We drill for masonry anchors instead, and because there is no wall cavity to hide a cable run in, wiring goes into a low-profile surface raceway rather than pretending a stud bay exists where it does not. Shared walls and building rules apply, and drilling into the exterior facade or a balcony structure is off the table without the board's sign-off, which is why we work inside the unit, not through it.

Pre-war and post-war houses in The Kingsway, Sunnylea and Mimico were mostly built with plaster over wood lath, not drywall, and it behaves differently under a drill. Many also have a brick chimney and fireplace as the natural TV wall, and since these houses predate mounting a television over the mantel, there is sometimes no outlet back there. Any new mains wiring or outlet needs a Licensed Electrical Contractor, separate from our low-voltage cable work.

Post-war bungalows in Alderwood, Long Branch and Rexdale are typically sided in aluminum or vinyl over narrower wall cavities than a newer build. Getting Ethernet or a mesh node up to a second floor means finding the cleanest existing path through those cavities instead of opening a wall on every run.

Etobicoke FAQ

Straight answers about AV and TV installation in Etobicoke

Can you drill into concrete walls in a Humber Bay Shores or Islington condo?
We assess actual wall construction, hidden services, anchors and building restrictions first. Where there is no usable cavity, a surface raceway may be appropriate. Structural or post-tensioned concrete requires the building's assessment and approval before drilling.
Does the condo board or property manager need to approve the install first?
Check with your property manager or condo board before booking. Many towers around Humber Bay Shores and Islington-City Centre West have their own rules about drilling into a shared wall and about booking the service elevator for the visit. We do not currently carry CGL insurance and cannot accept work where a certificate of insurance is required. Access and drilling requirements must be agreed before booking.
Can you mount a TV over a brick fireplace in a Kingsway or Sunnylea house?
We assess wall construction, fireplace clearances and the display manufacturer's temperature limits first. We do not route TV power cords inside walls. New mains wiring or outlets must be handled separately by a Licensed Electrical Contractor.
My house has plaster and lath walls instead of drywall, is that a problem?
It changes how we find a safe spot to drill, not whether we can. Plaster over wood lath, common in The Kingsway, Sunnylea and older Mimico houses, cracks differently than drywall and does not always sit on a stud where you would expect from the spacing pattern in a newer build. We locate the studs properly before anything gets drilled instead of guessing from a stud finder alone.
What about parking and getting into the building?
Tell us on booking if you are in a condo with visitor parking limits or a house on a street with permit-only parking, and we will plan around it. For towers, we also confirm elevator booking and any move-in window rules with you before the appointment, not after we are already parked outside.
How long does a typical TV mount or wiring job take in Etobicoke?
A standard wall mount runs 45 minutes to an hour and a half, the same as anywhere else in the GTA. Add time for in-wall cable concealment, for finding a real path through plaster and lath in an older Kingsway or Mimico home, or for a surface raceway on concrete in a Humber Bay Shores condo. Zone A bookings, which includes Etobicoke, are usually same-week.
What if the wall cannot actually support the mount?
We check before we drill, not after. On drywall we find the studs and use the anchor rated for what is actually there, on concrete we confirm the anchor is rated for the TV's weight, and if a wall genuinely cannot take a mount safely, whether that is failing plaster, hollow block, or a spot with no structure behind it, we will tell you and work out a spot that can, instead of hanging a TV on hope.
