Why people finally deal with it

The dish stopped working years ago — here's what's actually driving the call

Most calls come from one of four places. The house is going on the market and a dead dish bolted to the brick gable is not a look buyers' photos need. A home inspector or a roofer flagged the lag bolts holding the bracket as a spot where water has been finding its way under the shingles or flashing. The satellite provider got cancelled years ago in favour of streaming and the hardware just never came down. Or nobody's watching cable at all anymore and the dish on the roofline is doing nothing but collecting snow load and reminding you it's there.

The roof-leak reason is worth taking seriously even if the dish still technically works. A bracket bolted straight through shingles or brick with the original builder-grade sealant is fine for a year or two — most of these have been up for a decade or more, and old sealant shrinks, cracks and lets water track down the lag bolts into the deck below. We see it most on brick gables and steep roof pitches, exactly where satellite installers liked to put the bracket for a clear southern sky view.

Curb appeal is the other half of it. An empty dish arm hanging off a second-storey gable reads as "nobody's touched this house in years" to anyone walking or driving past — agent photos included. Full removal with colour-matched patching is the cheap fix for that: $179 against a listing-photo problem is not a hard call.

Full removal, step by step

Everything that comes off the wall, and everything that gets fixed after

Dish-only removal stops after step 2. Full removal — $179 — goes all the way through step 6.

  1. Dish off the mount. Unbolted from the bracket and taken down, wrapped if it's still in one piece.
  2. LNB and feed arm removed from the dish head.
  3. Bracket unbolted from the structure — wall, gable, roof deck or chimney, down to the last lag bolt.
  4. Coax cable pulled back to the entry point and capped, or removed entirely if you don't need it.
  5. Holes filled and sealed, then colour-matched to the brick, stucco, siding or shingle around them.
  6. Dish, bracket and hardware taken away for disposal and recycling — nothing left leaning against the fence.

Toronto FAQ

Straight answers about satellite dish removal

Will you leave the bracket, or take everything out?
Your call. The $99 dish-only removal takes the dish, LNB and cable off cleanly and leaves the bracket bolted to the wall, gable or roof. The $179 full removal also unbolts the bracket down to the structure and patches every hole it leaves, colour-matched to the wall. Most people selling the house or fixing curb appeal go full removal — most people just swapping to Starlink go dish-only.
Will the patch match the paint or brick colour?
That's the point of full removal. We fill the lag-bolt holes, then match sealant or touch-up paint to the brick, stucco or siding around them before we leave — not a white blob on a red brick wall. Exact factory-match isn't always possible on old or faded brick, but we get close enough that you have to know where to look for it.
Can you remove a dish from a condo or a two-storey brick gable?
Yes — a second-storey brick gable or a condo balcony rail with a dish nobody's used in years is most of what we remove. We carry ladders and fall protection for second-storey and gable work, and for condos we just need your board's go-ahead before we touch shared exterior surfaces.
What happens to the old dish — do you take it away?
Yes. The dish, LNB, arm and bracket all leave with us — nothing gets left leaning against your fence or put out for the city to deal with. Metal hardware goes for recycling and the dish itself goes to proper waste disposal.
How fast can this get done?
A dish-only removal is usually 20 to 30 minutes. Full removal with hole patching runs 45 minutes to just over an hour, depending on height and how many lag bolts are in the bracket. Zone A bookings are often same-week, and if you're bundling it free with a Starlink install we do both jobs in the one visit.
