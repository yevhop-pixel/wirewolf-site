Why wired

Wired cameras, installed by someone who runs cable for a living

Most “security camera” purchases in this city are a $40 wifi camera from a big-box shelf, stuck under the soffit with the tape it shipped in, streaming to an app that stops working the moment the internet does. That is a doorbell with delusions of grandeur, not a security system.

We install wired PoE (Power over Ethernet) cameras instead. One cable per camera carries power and video back to a recorder in your house — nothing to charge, nothing fighting your router for bandwidth alongside three phones and a smart TV, no picture that drops the moment somebody's microwave kicks on. It is the same category of hardware warehouses and banks run, sized for a house.

The install matters as much as the hardware. Your technician is a certified AV field engineer and control-system programmer, ex-Bell — cable is routed through the attic, wall cavities and soffit, not zip-tied along the eavestrough for the next windstorm to take down. Cables get labelled. Racks get documented. Every camera and every run gets recorded, so if you add a camera in two years or bring in a different installer, the system still makes sense to somebody who is not us.

Cloud cameras have an obvious weak point: cut the internet or unplug the camera and the footage goes with it. An NVR sits inside the house, out of sight, recording independent of your internet connection — if someone unplugs your router on the way in, the recorder keeps writing to its own drive the entire time.

How it works

Local recording, footage that is actually yours, cameras aimed at your property

Everything records locally to an NVR — a small recorder that sits in a closet or equipment rack in your house and writes footage straight to its own hard drive. It works with or without your internet connection, because the cameras talk to the recorder over your local network, not the cloud. If you want to check the cameras from your phone while you are at work, that is part of the standard setup, not an add-on, and it does not carry a subscription.

Cloud-storage systems flip that arrangement: your footage lives on somebody else's server, and you pay a recurring fee that climbs with every camera you add and every extra day of history you want kept. We will set one up if that is genuinely what you want, but for most houses local recording does the same job for a one-time cost, with no monthly fee required.

Where the cameras point matters as much as how they record. We plan camera angles at the walkthrough to cover your entrances, driveway and yard — your property, not the neighbour's kitchen window or the gap between your houses. It is not just good manners; it keeps the footage useful, because a camera aimed half at your porch and half at someone else's window does a worse job at both.

A camera system runs entirely on its own, or it plugs into a wider setup — locks, lighting, an alarm panel, motion-triggered scenes — controlled from one app instead of four. That side of the job is handled by an actual control-system programmer, not a box that syncs eventually. See the smart home installation page for what is involved.

Payment is due on completion — Interac e-Transfer or cash, nothing upfront. Camera hardware, cable and the NVR are billed at cost with the receipt shown where we supply them, and every mount and cable run carries our lifetime workmanship warranty. Full terms are at /terms.html.

Toronto FAQ

Straight answers about security camera installation

Wired or wireless — which should I get?
Wired, where the install allows it. Battery cameras look convenient until you are the one climbing a ladder every few weeks to swap batteries, and wifi cameras choke your router the moment three of them try to stream at once. We run PoE (Power over Ethernet) cameras on a dedicated cable back to a recorder — one cable per camera carries power and video, nothing to charge, nothing fighting your household wifi for bandwidth. Wireless still has a place on a shed thirty metres from the house where a cable run is not practical, and we will tell you honestly when that is the better call.
Do the cameras need an internet connection to work?
No. Local recording to an NVR (network video recorder) works whether or not your internet is up, because the cameras talk to the recorder over your local network, not the cloud. Internet only matters if you want to check the cameras from your phone while you are out — the recording itself keeps running through an outage.
What is the difference between NVR and cloud storage, and do I have to pay a monthly fee?
An NVR is a recorder in your house that writes footage to its own hard drive — you own it, there is no subscription, and nobody but you can access it. Cloud storage sends your footage to somebody else's server, usually with a monthly fee that scales with camera count and how long you want footage kept. We install local NVR systems by default, which means no forced monthly cost. Remote viewing on your phone is built into that setup at no extra charge — a subscription only comes into it if you specifically choose a cloud-based system instead.
I already bought cameras. Can you just install them?
Yes, in most cases. Text us the model and how many, and we will confirm NVR compatibility before booking. You pay labour and setup only — there is no hardware markup, because you are not buying the cameras through us. The one thing to flag is wifi-only cameras with no PoE or NVR output — we can still mount and wire those for power, but they will not feed into a local recorder, and we will tell you that before the job starts.
Where can security cameras legally point in Ontario?
This is practical guidance, not legal advice: point cameras at your own property — your doors, driveway, yard — not into a neighbour's windows, yard or the gap between your houses. A camera that catches a sliver of public sidewalk or street in passing is generally fine; a camera aimed straight into someone else's living room window is the kind of thing that brings a bylaw complaint or worse. We plan camera angles with this in mind at the walkthrough, and if a spot you want covered would mean aiming into a neighbour's property, we will suggest a placement that gets you the coverage without the problem.
How long does a 4-camera install take?
Most 4-camera jobs run half a day, from mounting the cameras and running cable to configuring the NVR and setting up remote viewing on your phone. Concealed runs through finished walls, a longer run to a detached garage, or awkward mounting heights can add time. We give you a realistic estimate at the walkthrough, not a guess.
