What changes the price

What actually pushes the number up — and what doesn't

Two-storey homes. A second-storey roofline doesn't change the $449 price — a roof or gable mount is the same flat rate no matter how many storeys the ladder has to clear. What it does change is which mount makes sense long-term: reaching a second-storey dish to clear snow every winter is a real hassle, and that's the one case where people upgrade to the $549 pole mount for reach alone, not because the roof job itself costs more.

Long cable runs. The flat rate includes a standard cable run from the mount to the router — typically a wall or two apart. Runs longer than that are $2 per foot, materials at cost, receipt shown. On a recent rural Milton property with two houses on it, wiring the second building meant a 150 ft run clipped along the facade instead of drilled through wherever was convenient, along with three mesh Wi-Fi nodes to cover both buildings — priced at the per-foot and per-node rates on this page, not folded into a vague "rural surcharge."

Trees forcing a pole mount. If the roofline can't clear the tree line, no amount of roof-mount creativity fixes that — the dish needs to go somewhere with open sky, which usually means a pole or non-penetrating stand. That's the $100 difference between $449 and $549, and it's the same $100 whether the obstruction is one maple or a whole tree line.

Metal roofs. Metal roofing changes the technique, not the price — we fasten into the panel rib with a butyl-sealed washer instead of through a flat pan, and the mount is still $449. The only way a metal roof adds cost is if you'd rather not put a fastener in it at all, in which case a pole or non-penetrating mount ($549) leaves the roof untouched.

Not included

What we charge is not what Starlink charges

Everything on this page is our price for installation — labour, mounts, cable, add-ons and optional hardware sourcing. None of it is the monthly cost of the Starlink service itself. That's a separate subscription, billed directly by Starlink to the account on your own card, and it has nothing to do with what we charge to mount the dish and wire the house.

We're deliberately not quoting Starlink's monthly plan pricing here — it's set by Starlink, not us, and a number we print today could be stale by the time you read it. Check starlink.com for current plans and pricing in your area before you book, and ask us if you want a second opinion on which plan tier actually fits how the property will use it.

Cost FAQ

Straight answers on Starlink installation cost

How much does Starlink installation cost in Ontario?
$449 flat for a standard roof or gable mount, $549 for a pole or non-penetrating mount. That covers the mount, a standard cable run into the house, and everything through to a working speed test. Travel is free across most of the GTA (Zone A) and a flat fee by zone everywhere else. The number is confirmed in writing before we book, and it doesn't change once we're on site.
Is the $449 really flat?
Yes. $449 is the price for a standard roof or gable mount on any roof type — asphalt shingle, metal or cedar shake — regardless of how long the job takes us. The only things that change the number are the ones listed on this page: a pole mount instead of roof ($549), cable beyond the standard run ($2/ft), mesh or Ethernet add-ons, travel outside Zone A, or dish removal if you're replacing an existing satellite. Nothing gets added at the door.
Do you charge for quotes?
No. Text us a photo of the roof and your postal code and we'll reply with a real number, not a "starting from" figure — usually within the hour. There's no charge to ask, no obligation to book, and no site visit required for most properties before we can quote.
What does travel cost outside the GTA?
Nothing inside Zone A — Toronto, Mississauga, Vaughan, Markham, Brampton, Richmond Hill, Oakville, Milton and the rest of the 0–50 km ring from our Etobicoke base. Beyond that it's a flat fee by distance: $59 for Zone B (50–120 km, places like Barrie and Hamilton), $179 for Zone C (120–220 km, Muskoka and Peterborough), and $299 for Zone D (220–350 km, up toward Kingston and Owen Sound). The fee is confirmed with your quote, not added once we're in the driveway.
Why do other installers charge $550–$1000 with a vague quote?
Usually because the quote isn't really a price — it's a starting point that depends on what the tech decides once they're on the roof. We publish $449 and $549 because those are the actual numbers for the two mount types, not a floor. The only variables that move the total are cable length, travel zone and add-ons like mesh or Ethernet, and all of those are priced on this page before you book, not negotiated on the ladder.
