Why Peterborough & the Kawarthas is different

Granite shorelines, farm sheds and a city that already has decent internet

Peterborough itself is not really the problem. Most of downtown runs on Bell fibre or Cogeco cable that works fine — if you are on a city street grid you probably do not need us. The gap opens the moment you cross into the townships: Smith-Ennismore-Lakefield, Douro-Dummer, Selwyn, and every cottage strung along the Trent-Severn from Chemong down through Buckhorn and up onto Stoney Lake. That is where we actually work.

The lake tree line. The Canadian Shield starts showing through around Burleigh Falls and Buckhorn — granite outcrops with mature white pine and hemlock rooted straight into the rock, sitting well above most cottage roofs on Stoney and upper Buckhorn Lake. A dish that clears the trees in July can lose signal to snow-loaded branches by January. We survey the actual sky view before we drill, and quote a gable, pole or short tower instead of installing something that will drop out every winter.

The farm country. East of Peterborough — Norwood, Havelock, Warsaw — the sky view is usually the easy part. The complication is the building: steel-roofed shops and bank barns never meant to carry a satellite dish. We carry the right fasteners and run a long cable back to the house rather than drill wherever is convenient.

The season. Most Kawartha lake cottages sit empty from Thanksgiving to May long weekend. Everything we mount on Chemong, Buckhorn, Pigeon or Sturgeon is installed to survive that unattended, and we show you how to shut the system down in the fall and bring it back up in the spring without a service call.

Peterborough & Kawarthas FAQ

Straight answers about lake-country Starlink

How much does Starlink installation cost in Peterborough and the Kawarthas?
$449 flat for a standard roof or gable mount, or $549 for a pole or non-penetrating mount. Peterborough and Kawartha Lakes sit in our Zone C, so add a $129 travel fee. That is the whole number — quoted in writing before we leave Etobicoke, not adjusted at your door.
My cottage on Stoney or Buckhorn Lake is surrounded by trees. Will Starlink even work?
Usually yes, but placement decides it. The granite shorelines on Stoney and upper Buckhorn carry mature white pine well above most rooflines. Text us photos of the roof and the tree line and we will tell you before booking whether a roof mount clears them or whether you need a gable, pole or short tower. If the site genuinely will not work, we say so and you pay nothing.
Should I mount on the roof or use a pole at my cottage?
Depends on the sky view, not the building. If the roof clears the tree line, a roof or gable mount is $449 and we are done in an afternoon. If the trees win, a pole or non-penetrating ground mount is $549 and gets the dish up and over them without new holes in a roof. We tell you which one you need from the photos, before you book either.
Can you get internet out to the bunkie, the boathouse or the dock?
Yes. A whole-property mesh add-on starts at $199 and covers the main cottage plus one or two outbuildings on the same signal. For a boathouse or bunkie set apart from the house — which is most of them on the Kawartha lakes — we run buried or weatherproof Ethernet at $120 per drop and place an outdoor-rated access point at the far end.
Can I split the travel fee with a neighbour?
Yes. Two installations booked in the same visit — yours and a neighbour's — save $100 off the second, and we only charge the Zone C travel fee once between you. Text us both addresses and we will pair the bookings.
